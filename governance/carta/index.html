Carta

Ontology

What is ontology, and why do we have it (whether we are deliberate about it or otherwise)? Since the beginning of civilization, after we first eked out comfort from the wilderness, we turned to the question of what it means for things or people to be true and valuable—in the sense of utility—so that they might sustain us and preserve our way of life with structural integrity, predictability, consistency, and general order.

One way of looking at the world is as an assembly of data points. Aristotle took to organizing and categorizing these data to make sense of reality. When such data are organized into tables or formally structured frameworks, we read them holistically, and patterns invariably emerge. In fact, even before assembling data, we intuit what is worth looking at and focus our attention accordingly. These patterns form a narrative, and it is this narrative that gives our lives meaning and purpose. This pattern in data is ontology: it presents an operational model of how the world functions as a mechanism. People may differ in how they interpret these data, but what ultimately matters is utility rather than consensus on meaning.

Consider the inukshuk. Any number people keenly aware of their surroundings will a stone - an aspect of reality or truth. Some will see a need, and then gather such stones to create inuksuit. Random data are brought to together to create a utility which then creates beauty - and not just the austere sculpture which when seen in foreign capital such as Washington or Tokyo takes us home.

It is their deliberate arrangement that transforms raw matter into an intentional landmark. Historically, inuksuit served as vital communication and survival tools across the treeless tundra—used to guide travelers, mark migration routes, indicate food caches, and signal dangerous areas, or built by hunters to mimic the human form and direct caribou herds toward enclosed areas. In modern times, the inukshuk has evolved into a symbol of connection, reminding us that structure and orientation (ontology) are foundational to navigating any landscape, physical or conceptual.

Invariably, we perceive meaning as a form of goodness or wholesomeness aligned with the natural world—the one place where we are all able to divine a sense of beauty. That beauty, while terrifyingly harsh and demanding, is nonetheless where we seek uncompromising home truths without sugar-coating.

There is a circularity to this question because it has been a long time since most of us emerged from the physical wilderness. Yet every so often, civilization collapses and must reconstruct itself from that primordial baseline. Today, we find ourselves immersed in a new, far more complex form of civilization that is itself possessed of a wildness. Call it, at least in relative terms, a chaos—one in which the organic and feral are in the midst of self-discovery within the bosom of a permissive secularism; an exploration of culture as defined by the individual in the midst of varied experience. Something which has taken the place of patriarchies we would almost universally consider coercive and oppressive. Now what we are aiming for is to find that mark, on either side of which is a variance within which we all sit in our various aspect. Each of us has a bespoke metric denoting eccentric Calling, while collectively we have a beacon shelling point which sets up for us a measure of consensus around which to turn as though round a maypole.

Meanwhile, the pathologically afflicted do as they please, helping themselves to whatever they like. In their own minds, they do what they must and take what they need—reasoning that if they didn't, someone else would, so it may as well be them. Elsewhere, apathy or parochialism causes people to turn away simply because it does not directly concern them or their own children. And yet, we all bear the costs—materially, and in our collective sense of what it means to be wholesome, well, and truly alive.

Look at a person without judgment, concerning what they need objectively. A person must feel wholesome, healthy, and in good order. They must feel sufficient in the present moment as an embodied, conscious entity seeking to sustain itself with essential vitality. This vitality is signaled by their presence as a unified identity possessing physiological and psychological well-being. Moreover, they must feel that they are upon a vector approaching alignment rather than diverging from it. However distinct or weird their personality appears against prevailing majoritarian standards, they must still feel embraced by society rather than alienated. For whatever they are and whatever they may appear to be, they remain an aspect and a microcosm of creation itself.

This is a desirable quality—one we may call soundness—whether possessed in any given moment through speed or stillness. Whatever the volume of externalized noise emitted, it is at least optimally configured within the bounds of what is feasible and fair under prevailing circumstances. Another way to conceptualize soundness is as a form of solidity: not excessively dense, but possessing a natural and organically appropriate sense of embodiment. If it should degrade, it does so almost as though through a natural weathering process—an oxidation that protects and secures what is held within, rather than through some extrinsic growth that invades and consumes the body, the body politic, and its capacity to achieve self-sovereignty through self-governance.

Ontology & The Social Network

As we seek to understand and model reality, we have language at our disposal as our primary utility. What I am endeavouring to build on this platform is an online utility operating at a foundational level: an ontology.

An ontology is both the philosophical study of being, existence, and reality, and a computer-science framework used to categorize and connect data. We create ontologies to navigate a shared landscape of reality—whether material or digital—as interpreted through the lens of individual human psychology.

You may have heard the phrase "life isn't fair, it's just fair game"—a coldly calculating realism that splits the world into two sets: players and the played. It suggests that opportunity is a scarce commodity naturally clustering within walled networks. The members of these enclaves rarely defend their privilege through merit; instead, they reduce their good fortune to a cold semantic binary of insider and outsider, accepting their inclusion as self-evident: that's unlucky for them, and lucky for me.

Good fortune, more than anything else, is a matter of opinion for the philosophical. But for the many, it is a notionally bounded space of reciprocated privilege and courtesy: the US dollar regime, a fraternal guild, or a too-big-to-fail corporate domain.

It doesn't have to be that way. In fact, I argue that it cannot be that way. Not if we are to function as a single planet with a semblance of common purpose. Not if we are to forge concord that sustains us indefinitely, ending the unfolding calamity of flood and pestilence afflicting our natural world order.

One is free to audit this ontology. In practical terms, this amounts to validating it—assessing its legitimacy and credibility by visiting it and engaging with its content personally, if one finds that a worthwhile endeavour.

This ontology represents truth as I personally see it—developed in clear, deliberate human language rather than compiled code. It seeks to function as an algorithm for aligning Alpha (individual success along a chosen path) with Omega (a holistically optimal outcome). That pursuit must inevitably pass through "left-handed" departure points (Beta), as unconventional paths now offer the only real frontier for discovering genuine opportunity outside mainstream dogma.

Regnum Dei

The core component of the ontology is Regnum Dei. Typically translated as the Reign of God, I render it as Divine Governance—where 'divine' retains its root sense of a search for truth. However, in the non-dualistic sense of reconciling pairs of opposites, using ontology to generate alignment may, in mythological terms, very well be seen as creating the Reign of God as it were to inform a form of anarchic governance on the social network."

The foundation for this ontology is well trodden. The triad of Satyam, Shivam, Sundaram—Truth, Goodness, and Beauty—reflects a universal cognitive architecture for modeling reality found across classical Western philosophy as the Platonic transcendentals, medieval scholasticism, and Eastern traditions. Whenever civilizations attempt to build enduring ontological frameworks, they invariably converge on these same three pillars because they address inescapable dimensions of existence: Truth provides the epistemic foundation and structural integrity, Goodness supplies the pragmatic and ethical utility for human action, and Beauty offers the resonant, systemic alignment that makes an existence whole and livable.

In material terms, this has been an industrial process spanning millennia—one of progress marred by occasional error. That error is something we must hold ourselves collectively responsible for. Until now, we have done this by socializing the costs, not least by externalizing them into our shared air, water, and soil as heat, noise, and whatever effluence accompanies it. This cannot go on indefinitely; we are each accountable for generating real, permanent solutions.

Regnum Dei comprises three elements required to create systemic autonomous peace, order, and good governance across our social network:

  • Intelligence (Sine Qua Non): The animating principle—anima and animus—without which no life can exist and hence indeed no reality.

  • Identity (Dial Square): If Semper Idem is the vessel, the work of life lived fully as an authentic persona processing interactions within context (community) and ecology (the Commons) is what is held within it as entirely unique.

  • Capital (E Pluribus Unum): The nature of value made so that it flows through the body politic to where it has most utility - where it enables productivity (creative thought and action)

The objective of any iterative process is to incrementally reduce error to yield an increasingly productive outcome. But true error reduction cannot be measured by human metrics alone: consensus must extend to all life, sentient and non-sentient alike, through the application of reason, arriving at an honest reckoning (price determination). This is done by accepting a singular semantic perimeter defined as a closed loop consisting of the planet's energy and matter, comprehensively governed through transparently integrated market mechanisms.

Peridot

Peridot is the vibrant green birthstone for August and the gem-quality variety of olivine. Seemingly possessed of an innate luminescence, here in this ontology it serves as the title of a series illuminating key terms that have either emerged or undergone transformation in our usage—making a reminiscence of original linguistic intention worthwhile. These terms are explored through my own personal experience and lens.

Canonical

Canonical provides a comprehensive glossary of terms, framing foundational social science concepts within the specific parameters of this ontology. Additionally, it introduces original theoretical shorthand designed to navigate and integrate contested ideas, providing space for perspectives that face friction or marginalization within contemporary Western discourse dominated by hegemonic state and corporatist interests.
